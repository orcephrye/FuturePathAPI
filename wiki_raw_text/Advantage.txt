Sometimes a Character will find themselves in a situation that makes performing a task easier or harder. For example, aiming at a target that is [[Conditions#Entangled|Entangled]] gives the person an advantage to there accuracy check. However, aiming at a target with an ultra-bright floodlight behind it would give the shooter a disadvantage. Advantage or disadvantage can be associated with an action to attack an opponent or apply to skill checks and saving throws. The most common use of Advantage is when a Player rolls an accuracy check with a weapon that a Character has [[Proficiency_Tree|proficiency]] with. 

An Advantage or Disadvantage is shown by adding or subtracting from a d20 roll. The +/- is determined by rolling an Advantage die. So whenever a Character has an Advantage or Disadvantage, the Player rolls '''1d20 +/- Advantage Die + (Other normal modifiers).'''

All Characters have an Advantage Die they can roll in these situations that represent a boost or detractor from their chances of success. When Characters level up, so does the Advantage Die. The die levels are based on a Character's [[Character Path|Path]]. At level 1, all Characters start out with either 0-1 (or d2-1) or a d2 Advantage Die. Advantage Die has levels as shown in the table to the right. Those levels are the levels of the Die '''NOT''' Character levels. Some characters start at AD Level 1 while others start at 2. Some characters at level 20 only have an AD level of 14, while some progress to Die Level 16. The table goes to level 20 because some abilities can increase the Advantage Die Level of a Character.

;Note: Almost all dice sets do not have a d2. So often a d2 is rolled using another die, where '''Odd''' values are 1 and '''Event''' values are 2.

Advantages and Disadvantages '''stack''' so if a Character gains more than one Advantage, then they gain more Advantage Die. The maximum amount of additional dice is 3. A high-level Advantage Die of 2dX is considered 1 Die or a single set of dice and thus only counts once against the maximum 3 dice. The same goes for Skill Rank Die. If a Character has to roll more than 3 Disadvantage Die in order to perform an action, then they cannot even try to perform the action.

Advantages and Disadvantages '''cancel each other out'''. So if a Character has both an Advantage due to flanking and a Disadvantage due to being [[Conditions#Shaken|Shaken]] then she doesn't roll any Advantage Dice.

There are all sorts of times an Advantage or Disadvantage may apply. The [[Combat#Advantages_and_Disadvantages_Phase_.28Step_3.29|Combat]] rules notes examples. Many different [[Conditions|conditions]] can affect a character's Advantage. Also, many items in the [[General_Equipment|General Equipment]] page provide bonuses or negate Disadvantages. Lastly, some Talents or [[Feats|Feats]] can affect a Character's Advantage.

'''Note:''' The 'shorthand' for Advantage Die is XAd. Example: 1d20 + 1Ad + Modifiers.

== Luck ==

'''Luck''' is a different way to improve a Character's chances and is more nebulous. The universe just shines on the Character, and they beat the odds, so to speak. 

When given Luck on a roll, the Character can roll the d20 twice and take the highest result. This is only for the d20. Unless the rules specify otherwise, only the d20 is rolled more than once. You can gain two levels of Luck. Or Extra Lucky roll, which simply means rolling 3d20 and taking the highest result. 

This is meant to suggest something more magical or otherworldly is at work. Something that is not explainable without getting into the metaphysical, or at least some would like to believe.

In turn, a Character can also be '''Unlucky''' and has the opposite effect. The Player rolls d20 twice and takes the lowest result. Same for Extra Unlucky.
