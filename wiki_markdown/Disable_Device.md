The ability to disable security systems, remove locks, and traps. As
well as disable robotics and other automated technologies.

## Check

  Lock Type (Example)                             DC
  ----------------------------------------------- ----
  Cheap (briefcase lock)                          15
  Average (home deadbolt)                         25
  High quality (business deadbolt)                30
  High security (branch bank vault)               40
  Ultra-high security (bank headquarters vault)   50

There are several examples of possible things that need to be disabled
or opened. This includes just about anything that needs to be turned
off, undermined, or otherwise stopped from functioning, and that also
has some level of security or preventive measures in place. Examples: a
locked door, a harmful trap, or some machinery.

The GM doesn\'t have to inform the Player of the check\'s success.
Sometimes it may be apparent that a door is unlocked or a robot stops
moving. But other times, such as with certain traps or when a robot is
programmed to fake it, the Character may not truly know whether they
succeeded until they try.

The Character cannot perform a check with a DC higher than 15 without
the proper toolkit.

### Open Lock {#open_lock}

A character can pick conventional locks, finesse combination locks, and
bypass electronic locks. The character must have a Lock Pick Kit (for a
mechanical lock) or an Electrical Tool Kit (for an electronic lock). The
DC depends on the lock\'s quality.

### Disable Security Device {#disable_security_device}

A character can disable a security device, such as an electric fence,
motion sensor, or security camera. The character must be able to reach
the actual device. If the device is monitored, the character\'s attempt
to disable it will likely be noticed.

When disabling a monitored device, the character can prevent their
tampering from being noticed. Doing so requires 10 minutes and an
electrical tool kit, and increases the DC of the check by +10.

  Device Type (Example)                             DC
  ------------------------------------------------- ----
  Cheap (home door alarm)                           20
  Average (store security camera)                   25
  High quality (art museum motion detector)         30
  High security (bank vault alarm)                  35
  Ultrahigh security (motion detector at Area 51)   40

### Disabling a Robot {#disabling_a_robot}

Disabling a robot is a full-round action and requires a successful
Disable Device check (DC 30). The robot must be pinned before the check
can be made.

Disabling an external cybernetic attachment is a full-round action and
requires a successful Disable Device check (DC 30). The creature to
which the cybernetic unit is attached must be pinned before the check
can be made. You cannot disable internal cybernetic attachments; this
requires a [Surgery](Medical#Surgery_.28DC_20.29 "Surgery"){.wikilink}
with the [Cybernetic
Surgery](Cybernetic_Surgery "Cybernetic Surgery"){.wikilink} Feat.

### Traps and Sabotage {#traps_and_sabotage}

Disabling (or rigging or jamming) a simple mechanical device has a DC of
10. More intricate and complex devices have higher DCs. If the check
fails by no more than 4, the character has failed but can try again.

If the character fails by 5 or more, something goes wrong. If it's a
trap, the character springs it. If it's some sort of sabotage, the
character thinks the device is disabled, but it still works normally.

A Character can rig simple devices to work normally for a while, then
fail after some time (usually after 1d4 rounds or minutes of use).

## Try Again {#try_again}

Yes, though the character must be aware that he or she has failed in
order to try again. A character can take 10 when making a Disable Device
check. A character can take 20 to open a lock or to disable a security
device, unless the character is trying to prevent his or her tampering
from being noticed. Disabling a simple mechanical device is a full-round
action. Intricate or complex devices require 2d4 rounds.

## Special

- Possessing the proper tools gives a character the best chance of
  succeeding on a Disable Device check. Opening a lock requires a Lock
  Picking Kit (for a mechanical lock) or an Electrical Tool Kit (for an
  electronic lock). Disabling a security device requires either a
  mechanical or an electronic toolkit, depending on the device\'s
  nature. The Character cannot perform a check with a DC higher than 15
  without the proper toolkit.
- A character with the [Cautious](Cautious "Cautious"){.wikilink} feat
  gets a +2 bonus on all Disable Device checks.
