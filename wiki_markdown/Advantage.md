Sometimes a Character will find themselves in a situation that makes
performing a task easier or harder. For example, aiming at a target that
is [Entangled](Conditions#Entangled "Entangled"){.wikilink} gives the
person an advantage on their accuracy check. However, aiming at a target
with an ultra-bright floodlight behind it would give the shooter a
disadvantage. Advantage or disadvantage can be associated with an action
to attack an opponent or apply to skill checks and saving throws. The
most common use of Advantage is when a Player rolls an accuracy check
with a weapon that a Character has
[proficiency](Proficiency_Tree "proficiency"){.wikilink} with.

An Advantage or Disadvantage is shown by adding or subtracting from a
d20 roll. The +/- is determined by rolling an Advantage die. So whenever
a Character has an Advantage or Disadvantage, the Player rolls **1d20
+/- Advantage Die + (Other normal modifiers).**

All Characters have an Advantage Die they can roll in these situations
that represents a boost or detractor from their chances of success. When
Characters level up, so does the Advantage Die. The die levels are based
on a Character\'s [Path](Character_Path "Path"){.wikilink}. Advantage
Die has levels as shown in the table to the right. Those levels are the
levels of the Die **NOT** Character levels. All Characters start out
with a Level 1 Advantage Die, which is d2. Character progression to
Level 18 Advantage Die. There may be some abilities (Talents,
Techniques, Feats) that can improve the AD Level.

Note
: Almost all dice sets do not have a d2. So often a d2 is rolled using
  another die, where **Odd** values are 1 and **Event** values are 2.

A Character can gain **Extra Advantage** also referred to as **Double
Advantage**. The maximum amount of Advantage is **Extra Extra** or
**Triple** Advantage. These can also be shorthand to 1AD, 2AD, or 3AD.
For 2AD, roll the Advantage Die twice and take the highest of the die
values. Then add the static Modifier twice. For 3AD, roll the Advantage
Die three times and then add the static Modifier three times.

Advantages and Disadvantages **cancel each other out**. So if a
Character has both an Advantage due to flanking and a Disadvantage due
to being [Shaken](Conditions#Shaken "Shaken"){.wikilink}, then she
doesn\'t roll any Advantage Dice.

There are all sorts of times an Advantage or Disadvantage may apply. The
[Combat](Combat#Advantages_and_Disadvantages_Phase_.28Step_3.29 "Combat"){.wikilink}
rules note examples. Many different
[conditions](Conditions "conditions"){.wikilink} can affect a
character\'s Advantage. Also, many items in the [General
Equipment](General_Equipment "General Equipment"){.wikilink} page
provide bonuses or negate Disadvantages. Lastly, some Talents or
[Feats](Feats "Feats"){.wikilink} can affect a Character\'s Advantage.

**Note:** The \'shorthand\' for Advantage Die is XAd. Example: 1d20 +
1Ad + Modifiers.

## Luck

**Luck** is a different way to improve a Character\'s chances and is
more nebulous. The universe just shines on the Character, and they beat
the odds, so to speak.

When given Luck on a roll, the Character can roll the d20 twice and take
the highest result. This is only for the d20. Unless the rules specify
otherwise, the d20 is rolled only once. You can gain two levels of Luck.
Or an Extra Lucky roll, which simply means rolling 3d20 and taking the
highest result.

This is meant to suggest something more magical or otherworldly is at
work. Something that is not explainable without getting into the
metaphysical, or at least some would like to believe.

In turn, a Character can also be **Unlucky** and has the opposite
effect. The Player rolls d20 twice and takes the lowest result. Same for
Extra Unlucky.
