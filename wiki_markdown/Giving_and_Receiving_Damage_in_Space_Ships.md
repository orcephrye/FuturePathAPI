affect combat. Single Crew Fighters,
[Hacking](#Hacking "Hacking"){.wikilink}, Counter Hacking, Drones, and
Special Weapon attributes are also included.

- **Battle Damage Difficulty Check**: = Xd10s rolled Y or higher. X =
  4 + **Structure** Mod, Y = 4 + **Shields** Mod.
- The total number of d10s used in a check is equal to the number of
  Hard Points activated for the attack. These d10s are called a **Die
  Pool**.
- A successful **[Scan](Scan "Scan"){.wikilink}** can add Ships

  Check.
- The successful d10s of a BD-DC can be re-rolled. This is similar in
  nature to the Exploding mechanic.
- The total number of re-rolls available to a Die Pool per turn is the
  Ship\'s **Weapons** Modifier number of times.
- A Ship can only receive 1 Battle Damage per round from Weapons Fire
  unless a Critical or certain Battle Damage is given.
- Space Ships can also be attacked electronically using **Hacking**
  actions.

## Giving and Taking Damage {#giving_and_taking_damage}

Ships do not have Hit Points (HP); instead, they take **[Battle
Damage](Battle_Damage_Chart "Battle Damage"){.wikilink}**. A ship can
only take so much Battle Damage before it becomes crippled and disabled.
This is a Ship\'s Battle Damage Capacity and is determined by [ship
size](Space_Ships#Hull_Sizes "ship size"){.wikilink} and is valued at 0
through 5. A ship can normally only take 1 Battle Damage per round of
combat from Weapons Fire. They can also take Battle Damage from a
Critical Roll, a special Battle Damage condition, or Hacking. Ships that
start with 0 Battle Damage gain the **Wrecked** status the moment they
receive any damage. A ship\'s Battle Damage Difficulty Check, or BD-DC,
is what a Weapon\'s Officer needs to roll against to confirm a hit.

During the **Action Phase**, the Weapon\'s Officer may attempt to deal
Battle Damage against 1 or more enemy ships. They can target as many
ships as they have active weapon Hard Points. A Weapon\'s Officer uses
d10s to roll against a target\'s **Battle Damage Difficulty Check** or
**BD-DC** (Also referred to as the target\'s Battle Damage Defense). The
default roll for Battle Damage is 4, 4, ie, roll 4 or higher on at least
4 d10s. The number of d10s a Weapon\'s Officer is allowed to use is
determined by the number of active weapon Hard Points committed to the
attack. These d10s form a **Battle Pool**. Before any re-roll can happen
in the Battle Pool, the Weapon\'s Officer may choose one failed die to
Boost with their chosen Ability Modifier. Likewise, if a Comms/Sesnsor
Office succeeded at a [Scan](Scan "Scan"){.wikilink}, they can boost one
failed d10 with their chosen Ability Modifier. d10s cannot be boosted by
more than one Player, and a successful d10 that has been boosted this
way cannot be rerolled.

A ship utilizes its **Structure** and **Shields** Core Attributes to
improve its defense. Structure increases the number of d10s that must be
rolled successfully. Shields increase the number of d10s that must be
rolled in order to count as a success. A ship with +1 Structure and +2
Shields would require an attack to roll a BD-DC of 5,6 or at least 5
d10s with a 6 or higher to succeed at delivering Battle Damage.

Likewise, a ship also has improvements to attack. A ship\'s **Sensors**
and **Weapons**. Sensors are the counter to Shields, while Weapons are a
counter to Structure. 1 + Sensor Modifier can be added to the result of
each d10 rolled if the Sensor Officer has done a successful **[Scan for
Ship Weakness](Scan "Scan for Ship Weakness"){.wikilink}** action
against the target that round. The Weapons Modifier informs how many
successful rolls within the Battle Pool can be rerolled for a chance at
another d10 success. Example: Sensor +1 would add +1 to all d10 rolls.
Weapons +2 would mean that if there were d10s that were rolled with
success, they could be rolled again. The total number of times a re-roll
can happen in the Battle Pool is up to 2 times, which can add to the
total possible successful d10s.

If the enemy ship is cloaked or in stealth and thus cannot be easily
detected, then the Sensor Officer\'s Scan also becomes an accuracy
check. So a [Scan](Scan "Scan"){.wikilink} acts as both an Accuracy
Check against the Target\'s Detection DC and a Scan for Weakness is
against the target\'s Tracking DC. If the Accuracy check fails, then the
Weapon\'s Officer cannot attempt an attack against that target.

### Criticals

A Sensor Scan can have a critical success. This is determined using the
Critical Success rules for 2d10s found
[here](Space_Ships#Ship_Conflict_Resolution "here"){.wikilink}. In
short: Critical success happens when at least 1 die explodes, and the
final result is greater than 20. Example: The player rolls 2d10 and gets
a 10 and a 5. The d10 with a 10 gets rerolled. It lands on a 6. Then it
is 10 + 5 + 6 = 21, and thus is a critical success. The Weapon\'s
Officer gains the following benefits when performing a BD-DC on the
target of the Critical Success [Scan](Scan "Scan"){.wikilink}.

1.  A successful d10 now counts twice.
2.  Can now reroll 1s. If the Weapon's officer can already re-roll 1s,
    this just adds to the range of numbers that can be re-rolled.
3.  If the Weapon's officer rolls enough successful d10s to count for
    twice the BD-DC +1, then the ship automatically gets another Battle
    Damage.
4.  The second battle damage is automatically considered a die roll of 7
    for the [Battle Damage
    die](Battle_Damage_Chart "Battle Damage die"){.wikilink} with no
    bonus to the repair DC.

### Battle Damage {#battle_damage}

Whenever Battle Damage is received, special effects may occur. The GM or
the Player of the Weapon\'s Officer rolls two d6s. This is called the
**Battle Damage Roll**. The more likely a number is to be rolled, the
lower the DC to repair the damage. So 7 is the lowest possible DC.
Battle Damage can cause side effects such as rocking a ship, dazing
characters, or exploding consoles. These side effects happen directly
after the Response phase. The Weapon\'s Officer gets to add modifiers to
the repair DC. This is Ship\'s Weapons Mod + Weapon's Officer's Wisdom
or Dexterity score (whichever they prefer).

### Repairing Damage {#repairing_damage}

During the **Response Phase**, the **Engineer** and **Medical Officer**
can act.

Repairing the Battle Damage takes two rounds, during which the Engineer
works on the problem. If the Engineer stops to do other work, they have
to start over. The DC is determined by determining the type of damage
using the Battle Damage Roll and consulting the [Battle Damage
Chart](Battle_Damage_Chart "Battle Damage Chart"){.wikilink}, and then
adding the attacking ship's Weapons Mod + the Weapon Officer's Wisdom or
Dexterity score (whichever they prefer). Repairing the Battle Damage
also removes any lingering side effects.

Side effects of Battle Damage can be repaired in 1 round. The repair DC
is the unmodified value provided by the [Battle Damage
Chart](Battle_Damage_Chart "Battle Damage Chart"){.wikilink}. All side
effects happen **after** the Response Phase unless specified otherwise.
So, for example, if the 2d6 Battle Damage Roll results in an 8, which is
a Blown Capacitor, the Weapons Officer has the chance to repair the
capacitor (DC10) before it blows a console. Or they can choose to focus
on the Battle Damage itself and hope it doesn\'t do too much harm.

The Medical Officer can help heal wounded Officers, replace an
incapacitated Officer, or negate negative effects. A Medical Officer
either has to move for one round to the location of the wounded Officer,
or the Officer has to move for one round to the Medical Bay. If the
Medical Officer knows that the Engineer isn\'t gonna resolve an Effect
that causes harm, they can get into position to heal next round.

Repairing damage to a specific ship system, like a Bay, or a Hard Point,
only requires a single check by the Engineer to fully restore it. The
check DC is 12 + Damage Modifier. If a Hacker did the same, it is DC
12 + the Hacker\'s Int Mod. If it were a Weapon system from a ship, it
is DC12 + the enemy ship\'s Weapon\'s Mod. Damage to a Ship\'s Core
System Attributes, like Engines for example, requires a Bill of
Materials and for the Ship to be out of combat. The Craft repair check
takes 2Minutes for the player to take 20.

### Wrecking SpaceShips {#wrecking_spaceships}

Once a Ship receives the same amount of Battle Damage as the Ship\'s
Battle Damage Capacity, it has zero Capacity left, and it is
**Crippled**. If the ship takes any more damage, its state becomes
**Deteriorating**. The damage is considered Deteriorating Damage instead
of Battle Damage and it is handled differently. Once the ship receives
Deteriorating Damage greater than the ship\'s total Battle Damage
Capacity, the ship starts **Exploding**. If the Explosion can be halted,
then the ship becomes **Wrecked**. The Ship can also enter **Wrecked**
if, while taking Deteriorating Damage, all the internal components are
damaged; the ship automatically enters the **Wrecked** state.

Crippled
: This status happens when the Ship has zero remaining Battle Damage
  Capacity. The ship receives the following penalties.

- -4 to Engines, -4 to Sensors, -2 to Electronics, -2 to Shields
- Disadvantage on all Pilot and Sensor checks.

Deteriorating
: This is when more damage is received beyond the Battle Damage
  Capacity. A Ship can only receive one Deteriorating Damage from any
  enemy activity with no exceptions. If the ship uses Hacking and
  Weapons fire, and even if it gains a critical and all of that
  succeeds, the ship still receives only one Deteriorating Damage from
  enemy activity. However, at the start of each turn, if the ship is in
  the **Deteriorating** state, it automatically gains another
  Deteriorating Damage before the first phase of spaceship combat
  starts. Deteriorating always has serious side effects. Read more about
  deteriorating, stabilizing, and side effects in the [Deteriorating
  Ships](Deteriorating_Ships "Deteriorating Ships"){.wikilink} page.

<!-- -->

Exploding
: This is when the ship has reached its maximum amount of allowed
  Deteriorating Damage (1 more than the Battle Damage Capacity). Or if
  the ship is Wrecked and is hit again by enemy weapons fire, which
  results in an Explode d6 roll ([more
  here](Deteriorating_Ships#Deteriorating_Effects "more here"){.wikilink}).
  The ship will explode in 1 round. The crew must race to the escape
  pods, beam off, or find another means of egress to survive. Treat this
  as a \'Change Position\' Ship Function for the whole crew. Crew that
  has already prepared to evacuate does not need to roll. The other
  option is for a player to use their Engineering/Science skills to
  repair or contain the Explosion. The DC for this is a flat 8 + Ship
  Size. If this happens, the Ship automatically enters the \'Wrecked\'
  status.

<!-- -->

Wrecked
: This is when a ship is completely non-functioning but still, mostly,
  in one piece. The ship has an Emergency Beacon that the Crew can
  choose to activate, or it can activate automatically. A Wrecked ship
  has the following penalties:

- Gain the \'Falling Apart\' Ship Quirk.
- 0 Engines, 0 Shields, 0 Sensors, 0 Electronics, 0 Weapons, 5 Structure
- All Hard Points are not functioning
- All Bays are not functioning
- Life Support is not functioning. (In Tech 1-2 Ships, this also means
  no gravity)
  - Only Emergency Life Support is working, with 2 hours before
    Characters start to die.

To recover from a Wrecked state, the Crew must make repairs. All Repairs
have a DC of 20 + (1/4 round up Encounter/Challenge Rating). A Repair
can be automatically successful if the Player chooses to take 20 and the
Character is able to meet the DC when rolling a 20 + other modifiers. To
recover Life Support, the ship must have some Electronics working and
either all Bays functioning or at least 6 Bays functioning.

#### Repairing Wrecked Ships {#repairing_wrecked_ships}

All repairs require either an Intelligence check or the Craft check
associated with that part\'s construction. Each repair, if rolled for,
takes 2 minutes, or the Player can choose to take 20 Minutes to
automatically succeed if they have the right modifiers. However, this
only counts when a Ship is in the Wrecked state. Once all the Hard
Points, Bays, and Core Systems are repaired, the ship goes back to
having zero Battle Damage Capacity without the Wrecked or Deteriorating
statuses. Normal damage done to a Ship or to a Bay/Hard Point through
[Specified
Targeting](#Specified_Targeting "Specified Targeting"){.wikilink} is
repaired using normal repair rules and requires a Bill of Materials. So
if a particular Hard Point was damaged by Specified Targeting during
combat, even if the Ship is Wrecked, the repair for that part requires a
normal Craft check and a Bill of Materials.

Whenever repairing a Core Systems Attribute like Engines, the first
repair check brings the Attribute to 5. A second repair will bring the
part to 8 (or to that Attribute\'s standard value if it is lower than
8). Any repair after that follows standard repair rules, requires a
Craft check, a Bill of Materials to be on hand, and only raises the
Attribute score by 1 point per successful check. In other words, without
Materials on hand, once a Ship is Wrecked, the highest its Attributes
can be is an 8 unless the crew manages to have or obtain enough
materials to fully repair the Ship.

A repaired, even fully repaired, Ship still has the **Falling Apart**
Quirk unless done at a Ship Yard or carefully (1 Day per repair, instead
of 20 minutes).

#### Taking Damage while Wrecked {#taking_damage_while_wrecked}

This is extremely bad for the already damaged ship. A Wrecked ship has
no functioning electronics and cannot be hacked. So any damage it takes
must come from external factors such as weapon fire. A wrecked ship is
immune to criticals. A wrecked ship is easily visible on sensors, with a
5 for both its Detection and Tracking DC, making it almost impossible
for an Enemy Ship to miss. If the opposing ship is hitting a Wrecked
ship, their Weapon\'s officer only needs to dedicate 1 Hard Point to the
action, and it is considered an automatic success. Whenever taking
damage a d6 is rolled and one of the following effects happens.

1.  A Player automatically dies.
    1.  The Players \'draw straws\' or roll for a Dexterity Save, and
        the lowest roller is killed or, at the very least, removed from
        combat and the ship. You can discuss the manner of death, or use
        a die roll. This matters because a person can be recovered and
        resuscitated under the right conditions.
2.  One Player gets hit for 1/4 of the character\'s level worth of their
    Hit Dice.
3.  1/2 the Crew, rounded up, gets hit for 1/4 of their Character\'s
    level worth of their Hit Dice.
4.  The ship re-enters the Exploding Phase at the start of the next
    round. (Gives two chances to react)
5.  The ship re-enters the Exploding Phase immediately. (Gives only one
    chance to react)
6.  Nothing. The impact hits a non-critical or already heavily damaged
    section.

## Combined Targeting / Swarm Combat {#combined_targeting_swarm_combat}

Combined Targeting is when multiple ships target the same Ship in the
hopes of beating its BD-DC. Each ship rolls as normal, including
re-rolls, and then adds the total number of successful d10s together in
a combined Battle Pool. This combined Battle Pool is what extra bonuses
or modifications can be added to.

Ships of different sizes add to the total with restrictions on how many
dice go into the pool. Pick the largest ship and add dice normally.
Smaller ships must subtract 1 successful d10 for each size they are
smaller. The minimum number of successful d10s a Smaller ship can add to
the Battle Pool is 1.

### Swarm Combat {#swarm_combat}

A Swarm is when a much larger ship is assailed by many smaller ones. To
be part of a swarm, a Ship must be more than 2 sizes smaller than its
target. A Swarm must have at least 4 total ships that qualify;
otherwise, they are just doing **Combined Targeting**.

In a Swarm, the attacking ships add all their d10s into the combined
Battle Pool to be rolled together. The Swarm applies the highest Scan
Bonus Modifier if applicable. For a Sensor Scan bonus to work, at least
1/2 of the total ships must make a successful Scan check, but the Swarm
applies the highest Scan Bonus Modifier available within the Swarm
(regardless of whether that ship was successful at a Scan check or not).
For the Weapon\'s Mod bonus, the available re-rolls are added together,
and a Ship\'s Negative Weapon\'s modifier doesn\'t count.

As with the size penalty, if a Ship is smaller than the largest ship in
the Swarm, then that Ship removes 1 Die from the Battle Pool for every 2
sizes it is smaller, with a minimum of at least one die. And 1 Weapon\'s
Modifier re-roll for every 1 size smaller; this doesn\'t create negative
re-rolls; it stops once it reaches zero added re-rolls.

A ship can perform Pilot maneuvers against the Swarm as a whole.
However, the sensor scan still covers only one ship at a time, and its
bonus applies to that ship\'s Point Defense if it has one.

## Specified Targeting {#specified_targeting}

Specified Targeting is when a ship attempts to target a specific part of
an enemy ship. For example, the ship\'s Cargo Bay, or its Engines. All
ship attributes, except Structure, can be targeted. All Hard Points can
be targeted unless specified otherwise. Any ship Bay that has an
external component, such as a Docking Bay or Cargo Bay, can be targeted.

Ship Attribute\'s 'hit points' is its Score. A Ship Attribute becomes
disabled once its score reaches 5. If it reaches 0, it is completely
destroyed and cannot be repaired in space.

Ship Hard Points, Bays have **8 hit points**. They are disabled after
losing all 8 hit points and are destroyed if they reach -8. Some things,
such as a Cargo bay, have alternative effects. Blasting a hole in a
docking bay to force your way in can also occur after all 8 hit points
are gone.

When doing Specified Targeting, the ship Structure doesn\'t count, but
Shields still do. Large size disadvantages also don't count when doing
specific targeting. (Small size disadvantages still do) Sensors are also
automatic. An accuracy/sensor check can still be attempted to see if a
Critical happens, but it isn't necessary for Sensors to be applied.
Every successful d10 roll counts as one damage against the specific
target. The maximum total damage any ship system can take per turn is 5,
unless it is a critical hit, in which case the max is 9. A Critical also
provides an instant 1 point of damage and allows rerolls like a normal
Crit.

## Flak / Point Defense {#flak_point_defense}

A ship can have a **Point Defense** system installed in one or more of
its Hard Points. These are designed to **counter missiles**, drones, and
ships 3 sizes or smaller. By default, Point Defense systems can launch 3
small missiles (relative to the ship) designed to counter fast, agile
targets. If used to attack Drones or small ships, they are treated like
normal weapons, except they can only target those enemies.

If used as Point Defense against incoming missiles, then whenever
missiles are fired at the ship, roll 3 (or more, depending on the Point
Defense Classification) d10s. Line up your highest roll with the
enemy\'s highest roll. Only the number on the die counts, no bonuses
from Sensors or other sources. If the die roll meets or exceeds the
enemy missile\'s die roll, both missiles hit and are destroyed;
otherwise, the point-defense missile is lost, and the enemy missile hits
its target. This continues with your second-highest roll against the
enemy\'s second-highest missile roll and so on.

A Point Defense system can only be used once per round. Special
attributes or Crew functions can allow other Hard Points to be treated
like Point defense; however, they only get to fire 1 shot (roll 1d10)
instead of 3.

### Attacking Swarms {#attacking_swarms}

The Ship simply targets the Swarm as a whole. The Swarm absorbs the
successful d10s. Take the easiest BD-DC first. If multiple ships within
the swarm have the same BD-DC, the Players in the swarm can decide which
ship gets hit. If the firing ship has more d10s than it takes to deal
Battle Damage to a ship, then it can spend those d10s on damaging
another ship in the swarm.

### Standard Weapons as Defense {#standard_weapons_as_defense}

If the Weapon\'s Officer has access to Advanced Functions, they can
perform [Defensive Fire](Defensive_Fire "Defensive Fire"){.wikilink},
which allows them to utilize standard Weapons as point defense. If the
Weapon\'s Office chooses to withhold using a Projectile, Laser, or
Plasma Weapon Hardpoint to attack, they can instead use one of these to
defend against incoming missiles. For each hardpoint, the Weapon\'s
officer chooses to use as defense, they gain 1d10. However, each
hardpoint takes a -2 to the die roll unless it is a Laser, which only
takes a -1.

Note about Lasers
: At Class 1 a laser can be used to both fire on an enemy ship and act
  as Point Defense. At Class 5, a Laser doesn\'t have a negative point.

## Hacking

**[Hacking](Hack "Hacking"){.wikilink}** is a Ship function available in
different levels of functionality to different Crew Positions. A Hack
can also do Battle Damage. However, only 1 Battle Damage per combat can
be caused by a Hack, and it takes 3 rounds to deal the damage. Each
round, the enemy ship has the chance to defeat the hack, forcing the
Hacker to start over. Hacking can also cause damage to consoles or ship
systems. Please review the [Hacking](Hack "Hacking"){.wikilink} page for
more information.

## Stealth

A Ship\'s size determines its Detection DC. However, a ship can attempt
to be stealthy by disabling its shields and other systems. This is
referred to as \'Silent Operations\'. It\'s Stealth DC is Detection DC +
Electronic Mods. This means a Ship\'s Shields are disabled. (It takes 1
round to turn them back on). It also means a ship cannot send out any
signals or perform any scans of its own, just receive signals or
messages. It also means a ship cannot use any Ship function that
requires Engines. A Ship can still move, but only slowly.

A ship has Sensors. The Passive Sensors are 10 + Sensor Mod. A ship
cannot target an enemy ship that it cannot passively detect. If that is
the case, the Sensor Scan check now serves both as an Accuracy Check and
a scan for weaknesses. If it fails, then the Weapon's Officer cannot
attempt to fire.

Ships can improve their Detection DC with Stealth modifications.

## Distance in Combat {#distance_in_combat}

There are distances in combat. **Short, Medium, Long, Huge, Vast,
Astronomical**. A ship from a Huge distance away gains a +2 to both
Detection DC and Tracking DC. At Vast, the bonus is +5, and at
Astronomical, it\'s +9. This means the targeting ship will need
increasingly advanced sensors to hit ships at greater distances. The
range is also limited by weapons, and this cannot be altered by the ship
itself. For example, lasers have a max range of Long and simply cannot
hit targets farther away.

Attempting to 'close or widen the gap' between the enemy vessel requires
a successful pilot check. Also, different ranges may take longer. To
switch between Short and Medium, or between Medium and Short, it takes
just 1 turn. To Medium to Long or Long to Medium takes 1 turn. From Long
to Huge or Huge to Long also takes 2 turns. From Huge to Vast or Vast to
Huge, it takes 8 turns. From Vast to Astronomical or Astronomical to
Vast, it takes 256 turns. (Basically, don't do it without FTL.)
Astronomical is considered to be roughly 1 AU in distance. FTL is not
usable at distances of roughly 1 AU or less. And it is dangerous to use
FTL to close the gap from Astronomical to something smaller. However, an
FTL upgrade can make Vast and Astronomical jumps possible and safer.

## Small Crewed Ships {#small_crewed_ships}

On ships with only 1 or 2 crew members, such as a fighter hull
configuration, there is no Captain. The pilot must choose which Phase
and Action to focus on. Switching Positions doesn\'t require a roll for
the Pilot of a Fine or Deminutive-sized ship.

Scanning on a Fine/Deminutive Ship
: Ships can have a Sensor/Targeting AI assistant. By default, any Fine
  or Deminutive size ship has a Targeting computer. This rolls a Sensor
  Scan automatically every turn with no bonuses. It also cannot scan to
  gather information during combat; it can only perform the Scan for
  Weakness. The Targeting system also cannot provide any additional
  bonuses to Weapons. If successful, the Ship Sensors Modifier +1 gets
  added to the d10s in the Battle Pool, and that is it.

<!-- -->

Weapons Fire
: The ship\'s weapons, if they are able to fire on a target, can only
  add their re-roll bonuses if the Pilot succeeds at their Pilot check.
  The Pilot can also choose to forgo any fancing flying and focus on
  Weapons fire and thus get to automatically add the Weapon\'s Modifier
  number of re-rolls and the Pilot\'s Dex or Wisdom Modifier to the
  Battle Damage DC bonus.

They can also choose to forgo actions in the \'Action\' Phase and choose to respond to a medical or engineering need of the ship. In this case, there is no Pilot check, nor do Weapons get a chance to fire. A Scan can still happen automatically, though.
