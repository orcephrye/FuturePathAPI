Crafting is dividing up into 5 sepreate skills: [Craft
(chemical)](-_Chemical "Craft (chemical)"){.wikilink}, [Craft
(electronic)](-_Electronic "Craft (electronic)"){.wikilink}, [Craft
(mechanical)](-_Mechanical "Craft (mechanical)"){.wikilink}, [Craft
(pharmaceutical)](-_Pharmaceutical "Craft (pharmaceutical)"){.wikilink},
[Craft (structural)](-_Structural "Craft (structural)"){.wikilink}. Each
of these skills is also **Rank Dependent**. The Rank depends on the
maximum Masterwork level an Item can be crafted in.

**Summary**: Crafting allows players to repair and make items. Crafting
an item requires [crafting
materials](General_Equipment#Crafting_Materials_and_Equipment "crafting materials"){.wikilink}.
The amount of materials is based on the price. You need 1/3rd of the
cost of the actual item in materials. This is called the **Bill of
Materials**. The crafting skill needed to craft an item is noted by the
item. For [Weapons](Weapons "Weapons"){.wikilink} it is by default
Mechanical, [Armor](Armor "Armor"){.wikilink} it is Structural, and for
[General Equipment](General_Equipment "General Equipment"){.wikilink} it
is listed at the top of each category. To repair an item, you need a
craft rank of 1. To make an item, you need a craft rank of 2. To
\'Uptech\' an item, you need a craft rank of 4, as well as 1 level in
the [Item Crafting
Branch](Proficiency_Tree#Item_Crafting_Branch "Item Crafting Branch"){.wikilink}.
To masterwork an item, you need a craft rank of 6, as well as 1 level in
the [Item Crafting
Branch](Proficiency_Tree#Item_Crafting_Branch "Item Crafting Branch"){.wikilink}.
Masterworked items require advanced crafting materials. For every
additional level of Masterworking provided to an Item, the Character
needs 3 more Ranks in the appropriate skill.

    :Note: Crafting in most fantasy-themed pen and paper games has a large list of crafting skills, which include things such as visual art and writing. This is a world where not everyone is educated or literate. Where artisans have specialized crafts that take 1/2 a lifetime to learn and the other half to train a successor. However, modern civilizations likely have their kids learn most of those things in schools. So in this universe, crafting is strictly focused on creating and repairing useful equipment, ships/houses, etc. For advanced writing of manuscripts, artwork, and so on, pick a profession.

## Checks/Actions

The Craft Skills are used for two checks. For **Creating** items or for
**Repairing** items. Both Creating and Repairing have two aspects: the
**Bill of Materials** and the amount of **Time**.

Craft skills are specifically focused on creating or repairing objects.
To use a Craft skill effectively, a character must have a crafting
[Kit](General_Equipment#Profession_Kits "Kit"){.wikilink} specific to
the craft (i.e: A Chemistry Kit) or some substitute for them. Without a
kit, a Character either cannot craft the item or takes double
disadvantage. The Character also cannot attempt to make a master worked
item without the correct kit. An Advanced Crafting kit can provide an
advantage bonus to crafting items.

Review each Crafting skill for more information.

## Crafting Costs {#crafting_costs}

Both Crafting and Repairing require materials. This is called the **Bill
of Materials**. Each Craft skill is associated with two types of
[materials](General_Equipment#Crafting_Materials_and_Equipment "materials"){.wikilink}.
**Normal** and **Advanced**. Advanced is usually required for complex
equipment or master-worked items. Advanced is always 3 times as costly
as Normal material.

In order to size up how much material you have on hand you can use
**Units** just like Cargo Space for [Space
Ships](Space_Ships "Space Ships"){.wikilink} or simply state how much
you have in cost. (IE: I have \$100 bucks left in Chemical materials). 1
Unit of materiel is roughly 100Lbs and 1x1x1 square feet of volume. The
prices for 1 Unit of Normal Material is:

- Chemical: \$250
- Electronic: \$100
- Mechanical: \$150
- Pharmaceutical: \$500
- Structural: \$100

Items require 1/3 of their price in Normal materials to make. Repairing
costs 1/10th of the item\'s price in material per **Battle Scars** it
has. You do not have to buy Crafting Material in Unit increments;
however, it is suggested in games where Players play with strict rules
on how much a person and/or ship can carry.

For Advanced materials, you have to buy the same amount of materials
that would equal half the cost of the item you are crafting. So if you
want to craft an item worth \$300 with basic materials, you buy \$100
worth of Normal materials or \$150 worth of Advanced Materials.

## Craft Checks {#craft_checks}

When the player has the correct crafting skill and rank for the item.
(The item should specify the crafting skill/rank) Then the Character can
attempt to make it. This requires a craft skill check to make sure the
Character doesn\'t make a mistake in building the item. The default
crafting DC:

- General Equipment - DC12
- Weapons and Armor - DC14
- Spaceship Hull and Parts - DC12
- Spaceship Accessories - DC14

Other rules:

- Masterworking by default adds +4 too DC per Masterwork level
- Tech Level does affect crafting DC, however, Up Teching does. Each
  level of technology improvement to an Item increases its Craft DC by
  +4.
- The first time an Item gains a Masterwork level, it gains +1 Procure
  Difficulty. This should only happen once.
- Non-critical failure means that the item is not made, and 1/2 of the
  materials are wasted, and 1/2 the time is also wasted before the
  person realizes the mistake.
- Critical failure, which is a natural crit of 1, results in 100% of the
  materials and time being wasted.
- For every 4, the DC is beaten, save 10% (round up) of the \'Bill of
  Materials\' cost. Maximum savings is 50% off the \'Bill of Materials\'
  total original cost.
- A Character cannot take 20 on Craft checks.

### Crafting Time {#crafting_time}

All crafting time is in 1-day increments. 1 day implies 8 to 10 hours of
labor that will leave a Character with the
[fatigued](Conditions#Fatigued "fatigued"){.wikilink} condition and
require sleep to remove. If the Character then continues to do
adventuring that same day before resting, the Character gains the
[exhausted](Conditions#Exhausted "exhausted"){.wikilink} condition by
the end of the day.

A personal item is an item designed for use by a single character. If
the **personal item** doesn\'t specify the time, then the **default
crafting time is 2 days** unless it is \'Large\', \'Gigantic\', or
\'Heavy\' then it takes **5 days** to craft. The Character can choose to
pause work. Or only work for half the day in order not to gain the
fatigued condition.

The Character can do things to reduce the time. Crafting facilities can
do part of the crafting work automatically for the Character. They cost
money to gain access, unless, of course, the Character owns one. These
workshops can allow the character to either build the item much faster.
Or have to work less every day. A basic workshop reduces the time in
half. So, for an item that takes 5 days to construct, it now takes only
2 days. Or 4 days of half-day work.

If a Character succeeds at the crafting check by at least 5, then the
Character can choose to reduce the time by 1 day for each 5 they exceed
the DC by instead of saving in the \'Bill of Materials\'.

Some items are tiny or diminutive from the General Equipment page. It
would seem silly that such small things should take 1 or more days to
craft. So General Equipment has an exception. General Equipment takes 24
hours for Medium-sized objects; every size larger takes an additional 24
hours or 1 day of labor. Smaller sizes reduce the time spent crafting. 1
day is meant to represent 8 to 10 hours. So a Small item should only
take 6 hours. Each size smaller reduces the time by 2 hours. The Minimum
time is 20 minutes.

Any item that is large enough to contain a person, a vehicle, for
example, requires a Workshop and a minimum of 15 days of work minumum.

A Character cannot take 20 on Craft checks.

### Helping Craft {#helping_craft}

This follows the [Aiding
Another](SkillsBasics#Aiding_Another "Aiding Another"){.wikilink} rules
for skills. With the following notes.

- Standard Aiding allows the main Character performing the crafting task
  to gain advantage per aid up to x3 and also the ability to re-roll
  failures for each aiding character up to 3 times. This represents
  other Characters helping the main Character avoid mistakes they might
  otherwise miss.
- With Cooperative Check, this implies that the work is divided evenly
  between the Characters and they no longer gain fatigue status as they
  do not work throughout the day. However, they can work \'around the
  clock\' to speed up production by up to x3 with up to 2 additional
  people helping. In this case, each helper gets an 8-hour shift during
  the day and gains
  [Fatigue](Conditions#Fatigue_Track "Fatigue"){.wikilink} as usual. It
  is up to the Players to determine which option they take.
- Only Structural crafting, or Gigantic Items, can take advantage of
  Dividing up the Task. This speeds up production and reduces wasted
  material. A failure here only wastes a portion of the \'Bill of
  Materials\' and doesn\'t require everyone to restart. Only one
  person\'s work. Dividing up the Task requires a workshop. Large
  structural items such as a spaceship, house, or boat take much more
  time and \*require\* workshops or some form of facility such as a
  docking bay or a shipyard. All of these are able to take advantage of
  \'Dividing up the Task\' rules.

Example: You have 4 players who wish to build a Ship together. All
players rolled, but 1 failed the check. If the Ship takes 100days to
build, then it can be built within 25 days, but with 1 failure after 25
days. 50% of the 25% of the \'Bill of Materials\' used by the player who
failed their check is lost, and the 4 Players have to take this work on
again. However, they can divide the remaining person\'s task among 4
people again and turn 25 days of labor into 7 days each (25 / 2, rounded
up). Do this until the ship is complete.

## Repairing Checks {#repairing_checks}

Repairing has the same DC rules as crafting. With a few changes. The
time is only 2 hours for personal items, weapons, armor, and general
equipment of Medium size or smaller. 4 hours (or 1/2 day) for larger
personal items. The \'Bill of Materials\' too repair the item is 1/10th
the normal \'Bill of Materials\' crafting cost per Battle Scar removed.

Procure Diff has no effect on materials or costs during repair. There is
a feat [Jerry Rigging](Jerry_Rigging "Jerry Rigging"){.wikilink} that
allows a repair without the cost of the materials but the DC is +6.
